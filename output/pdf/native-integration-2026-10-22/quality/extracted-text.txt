ConseqaX: An Evidence Protocol for Recovering
Ambiguous Agent Actions
Devrajsinh Jhala
Independent Researcher
Abstract—A timed-out tool call can leave an agent uncertain
whether an operation failed or will commit later. ConseqaX binds
temporal contracts, readback witnesses separate from acknowl-
edgements, and distinct state and action-origin conclusions to an
application recovery policy. We evaluate five deterministic policies
under shared provider guarantees. Study A retains all 15,360
SQLite worlds across 24 authored cases in 12 purposive families;
its 128 seeds added no recorded-metric variation within a case–
policy group. Four readback policies achieved safe recovery in
14 cases, versus eight for response-driven retry. The package
matched a strong reference with separate evidence arithmetic and
shared application transitions. Its original-contract state reports
were wrong in 0/2,304 definite outputs, while harmful effects
remained possible. Polling doubled reads without improving final
claims under these schedules. A separately frozen 960-world
follow-up varies 64 availability profiles across three domains:
polling resolves 27 profiles versus one for a single final read,
at 741 versus 192 total reads per policy. The reference shares
this gain; physical outcomes remain identical. The contribution
is an inspectable evidence protocol and conditional coverage–cost
evaluation. A postcollection packaging deviation in Study A is dis-
closed. These internal experiments do not establish autonomous-
agent superiority or transfer to independently operated services.
Index Terms —agent systems, outcome verification, recovery,
idempotency, evidence provenance, uncertainty
I. INTRODUCTION
An agent requests an inventory allocation, ticket, or pub-
lished artifact. The provider accepts the operation, but the
acknowledgement times out. An immediate retry may create
a second record; stopping may leave the task unfinished. A
separate read can reveal an existing effect, yet even a fresh
empty snapshot cannot establish that the first request will never
commit later. A reliable recovery decision depends on the
operation’s identity, provider guarantees, temporal target, and
available evidence, rather than the response alone.
Three distinctions matter. First, a response describes a
tool interaction, whereas a readback supplies evidence about
external state. Second, global task satisfaction differs from an
effect being linked to this agent’s operation. Another actor can
complete the task; missing actor metadata need not imply an
external actor. Third, a hindsight-correct assertion can have
been unsupported when issued. A controller that guesses the
eventual outcome correctly has not thereby justified a retry or
a completion claim.
These are established systems and verification concerns
[1], [ 2], [ 3], [ 4], [ 5], [ 6]. ConseqaX makes them explicit
in a reusable evidence protocol [ 7]. Its typed records bind
contracts, attempts, responses, observations, and verification
receipts. Admitted readback can support satisfied, violated, or
unknown state, while origin remains a separate conclusion.
The protocol does not authenticate a provider or manufacture
request finality. Applications supply source semantics and the
policy connecting receipts to actions.
This paper centers that policy boundary. We com-
pare response-driven same-key retry, state-only readback,
a separately implemented provenance-aware reference, a
package-backed single-final-read ablation, and package-backed
bounded polling. Controller actions change subsequent SQLite
effects and observations. The experiment asks whether the
declared evidence policy changes safe recovery, final-report
correctness, or definite-report coverage at a measured read/wait
cost, and when ordinary provider idempotency removes the
apparent advantage.
The research questions are falsifiable. Q1: How does polling
change safe recovery relative to response-driven retry, to-
gether with own harmful effects and recovery failures? Q2:
Does the package-backed implementation agree with a same-
information reference using separate evidence arithmetic? Q3:
What additional origin-report coverage and correctness appear
beyond state-only reporting? Q4: Does continued polling
change final-report correctness and coverage relative to one
final read at its additional resource cost? Q5: What abstention
and definite-error tradeoffs remain under incomplete public
histories? Equal or worse recovery, incorrect conclusions,
unnecessary reads, or parity under shared provider guarantees
can limit the proposed benefit.
The contribution is a specified application protocol, a
bounded recovery policy, and a paired closed-loop evaluation
of these questions. Separately implemented reference parity
would support observed conformance, without demonstrating
superiority. Because state-only and provenance-aware variants
share action transitions, their comparison isolates reporting
rather than a provenance-caused recovery benefit. The 12
purposive families provide challenge-suite evidence; repeated
seeds do not establish a population of independently sampled
tasks. A separately audited native development execution and
the prospective external comparison are reported in Section VI ,
without a comparative benchmark claim.
II. RELATED WORK AND POSITIONING
Idempotence and eventual consistency are longstanding sys-
tems concerns [ 1], [ 2]. Verified Tool Calls separates acknowl-
edgements from independently checked effects and admits
unknown postconditions [ 3]. Callability Is Not Operability

examines controlled interface ambiguity and terminal agent
claims [4]. Agent-tool anomaly work distinguishes runtime ob-
servations from external events [5]. LIMBO grades committed-
effect histories and studies late commits [ 6]. Consequently,
response/effect separation, effect ledgers, and the danger of
ambiguous retries are foundations and comparison require-
ments here, rather than priority claims.
PROV-DM represents entities, activities, agents, and respon-
sibility [ 8]. ConseqaX is neither a new general provenance
model nor a PROV conformance implementation. Its narrower
boundary links operation identity, temporal contracts, individ-
ual observation witnesses, and explicit unknown verdicts. A
source-supported origin link is not proof that the action was
counterfactually necessary.
Causal Agent Replay (CAR) performs same-policy interven-
tions with contrastive suffix and permutation Shapley attribu-
tion [9]. Policy-Conditioned Counterfactual Credit studies ver-
ified outcomes under declared interventions and continuation
policies [ 10]; CausalLoss-Fin jointly intervenes on decisions
and infrastructure messages [ 11]. Those questions differ from
whether a live recovery action is justified by its available
evidence. Our earlier replay adapter uses existing estimators.
The present experiment requires neither counterfactual writes
nor a new attribution method.
AppWorld, ToolSandbox, and 𝜏-bench already evaluate
stateful agent interaction and world outcomes [ 12], [ 13], [ 14].
A maintained retail/airline corpus and native DB evaluator also
support our earlier model-free transfer experiment [ 15], [ 16].
Replaying reference actions or executing deterministic recov-
ery logic does not supply an autonomous benchmark score.
Section VI separates native integration evidence from the
planned comparison; broader recovery validation still requires
native reward, matched strong controls, and independently
checked effect histories under relevant faults.
The present contribution is therefore conditional: an ex-
ecutable evidence contract and an auditable comparison of
policy tradeoffs under declared capabilities. Its value must
come from the recovery behavior, final-report correctness, cov-
erage, and cost actually measured, rather than from attributing
established distributed-systems guarantees to a framework.
III. EVIDENCE AND RECOVERY PROTOCOL
A. Objects and temporal targets
A logical action has stable identity and payload; each
dispatch has a distinct attempt identity. Captured records
include the request, provider response, reported assessment,
separately captured observation, and resulting verification re-
ceipt. Reported assessments are outputs, not measurements
of hidden reasoning. An observation states its source, scope,
completeness, availability, observation time, watermark, and
effect history. The application declares which source and clock
are authoritative.
Let registration be 𝑡0, the original deadline 𝐷, stability
duration 𝑊, and original horizon 𝐻 = 𝐷 + 𝑊. The original
contract remains fixed. A separate recovery deadline 𝑅 ≥ 𝐻
and safety horizon 𝑆 ≥ 𝑅 define later recovery in this study: its
matching candidates must commit by 𝑅 and persist through 𝑆,
and its count is checked throughout [𝑅, 𝑆]. A later completion
may satisfy recovery while violating the original SLA. Neither
a new read nor an explanatory receipt moves 𝐷.
For the exact-count record contract, a candidate matches the
expected business fields, commits within [𝑡0, 𝐷], and persists
through 𝐻. The declared count must hold, including the
maximum simultaneous matching count over [𝐷, 𝐻 ]. Histor-
ical removals and transient extras therefore matter; a current-
row snapshot alone is insufficient. Simultaneous commits and
removals are evaluated together, with boundary conventions
fixed before collection.
The verifier concludes satisfied, violated, or unknown. A
complete admitted observation must cover the registered scope
and history through 𝐻. Stale watermarks, inadequate scope,
unavailable reads, or malformed history retain unknown.
Source identity, completeness, timestamps, and provenance
are trusted adapter declarations. Schema checks and hashes
establish structure and retained bytes, not source authenticity
or transactional guarantees.
B. State, origin, and support
Global state satisfaction does not require this agent to have
produced the effect. Action-linked satisfaction additionally
requires qualifying target evidence linked to the logical op-
eration. A known global violation makes this linked contract
false even if an own matching record exists: a second effect can
invalidate an exactly-one task. Mere witness presence cannot
replace the whole contract.
A matching action identity with absent or matching actor
metadata can support linkage under the admitted provenance
law. Explicitly different identities exclude this operation; miss-
ing or conflicting linkage can remain unknown. Provenance
does not turn an externally satisfied state into a failure. Each
observation retains its own witnesses. A later record sharing
an effect ID cannot inherit an earlier observation’s origin link
while silently changing payload, actor, or removal history.
Hindsight correctness and evidence support are distinct
questions. The present evaluator scores final state and action-
linkage reports against full-ledger truth, with separate definite-
report and abstention counts. It does not independently label
support at issuance or score safe-to-retry assertions. Retained
public histories permit automated checks against the declared
admission rules; a hindsight-correct guess alone does not es-
tablish support. An origin abstention does not erase a definite
state conclusion. Conditional error among definite assessable
reports and correct definite yield over all planned worlds
answer different questions.
C. Why historical absence cannot resolve a pending attempt
Conditional retry boundary. Suppose an operation times
out without atomic deduplication, terminal resolution, com-
pleted cancellation, or an enforceable in-flight bound. Let
𝐿 < 𝑅 be the last useful retry time. T wo provider worlds give
identical admissible histories through 𝐿: the initial attempt
never commits in one and commits after 𝑅 but by 𝑆 in the

other. A retry by 𝐿 achieves recovery by 𝑅; a later retry
cannot. An extra effect is harmful even if compensated, and
no alternative operation achieves the target.
A deterministic policy takes the same decision in both
histories. Retrying creates a duplicate in the late-commit
world; declining misses recovery in the never-commit world.
For a randomized policy, couple its randomness and let 𝑝
be its useful-retry probability. Duplicate risk in the second
world is at least 𝑝 and missed-recovery risk in the first at
least 1 − 𝑝. Their sum is at least one for this constructed
pair. This elementary indistinguishability argument states a
known systems boundary [ 6], rather than a new impossibility
or a measured prevalence. Provider guarantees can break its
assumptions.
D. Application safety gate and bounded reads
Every policy preserves the operation key and payload.
When a public native idempotency capability is admitted, the
provider atomically reserves pending and completed work for
that key. All readback policies may use the same-key exception
to retry within the common cap before 𝐷. The response-driven
arm also benefits from the identical provider semantics; it
is the ordinary bounded idempotent-retry comparator in that
stratum.
Without that capability, the readback policies permit another
dispatch only when every previous attempt has an admitted
terminal-no-effect guarantee, fresh complete trusted readback
establishes no relevant effect through the decision time, budget
remains, and time is strictly before 𝐷. A newer terminal
response does not resolve an older ambiguous request. An
empty watermark proves only past absence; it supplies no
terminal guarantee. Resolution of the agent’s attempts also
cannot prevent another actor’s future write. Global race free-
dom would require additional provider semantics.
The package’s generic retry advice is recorded, but this
application gate authorizes dispatches. Provisional predeadline
verification has its own target ending at the current time;
it never replaces the fixed 𝐷/𝐻 receipt. Bounded polling
uses externally scheduled callbacks rather than the public
blocking polling helper, allowing all arms to share admitted
opportunities. At exhaustion, insufficient evidence remains an
abstention. No policy dispatches after 𝐷; recovery at 𝑅 is a
distinct evaluator outcome, not an extension of the original
dispatch window.
IV. FROZEN CLOSED-LOOP STUDY
A. Corpus, separation, and execution
The schedule crosses 24 new cases, 128 timing seeds, and
five policies: 15,360 planned worlds. Cases span 12 purposive
families in three nonpayment SQLite domains: inventory, tick-
ets, and artifacts. Related cases share mechanisms; identifiers
and seeds do not create new families. Separate AI agents
authored controllers and cases. The controller author never
inspected the case file, and the lead integrator did not manually
inspect case values before freeze. This is procedural separation
within an AI-assisted workflow; the tasks are not externally
authored or randomly sampled from a task population.
Each world uses a fresh database. The frozen exogenous
event schedule, timing seed, provider law, source behavior,
budgets, and read opportunities are paired across policies.
Actions create branch-specific effects, so later observation
contents can legitimately diverge. Matching means equal ca-
pabilities and opportunities, not forcing one policy to consume
another’s realized evidence. Readback is a separate application
method on the same SQLite connection and ledger. It supplies
a signal separate from the acknowledgement, rather than an
independent connection, source, or trust domain.
The public allowlist includes request/contract, identi-
ties, response history, provider capability and terminal re-
ceipts, and scoped readback history. Controllers receive no
case/family/fault labels, hidden future events, evaluator truth,
or another policy’s reports. They are deterministic application
state machines. The run requires no LLM, user simulator,
remote provider, or paid API call and supplies no autonomous-
agent benchmark reward.
B. Five policies and the strong comparison
The single-read arm is not forced to spend its final query
before 𝐻. It may perform a predeadline gate query when
terminal guarantees make retry relevant; its first query at
or after 𝐻 is its only final query. Polling can use later
opportunities to resolve lag, incompleteness, or unavailability.
Equal maximum budgets need not yield equal spend; early
stopping and skipped queries are policy outcomes.
The reference imports no package verifier. The controller
author implemented its evidence arithmetic separately, while
sharing the declared application transition logic with the
polling comparison. Thus exact agreement is a conformance
result, not a contest between independently designed recov-
ery algorithms or external implementations. State-only and
provenance-aware arms likewise do not test a provenance-
caused action benefit when transitions coincide. They test
origin-report coverage and correctness under the admitted
provenance law.
C. Endpoints, costs, and finite-suite analysis
The primary all-slot endpoint is operational safe recovery:
the global recovery payload/count contract holds by 𝑅 and
persists through 𝑆, with neither an own duplicate nor an
own unwanted effect committed through 𝑆. An own duplicate
means more cumulative own committed effects than the al-
lowed logical-operation count, including effects later removed.
An own unwanted effect mismatches the expected business
fields or commits after 𝑅. These predicates do not certify
the absence of every harmful external activity; another actor’s
effects can still violate the global recovery count. Every sched-
uled cell remains in the operational denominator, and failed or
unstarted execution cannot earn success. Failed cells’ semantic
state, origin, and harm outputs remain unavailable rather than
receiving fabricated violation labels; an incomplete collection
cannot yield a complete audited result. Own duplicate and

V. RECOVERY RESULTS
A. Retained collection and effective diversity
All 15,360 scheduled worlds are retained, with 3,072
completed cells per policy and no execution failures. The
unchanged auditor verified every SQLite snapshot and re-
computed the endpoint summary; a portable artifact recheck
agreed. The packaging deviation in Section IV-D remains part
of this result. Across all 120 case–policy groups, none of the
recorded endpoint or resource metrics varied over the 128
seeds. Some exogenous timestamps varied, but the repetitions

added no endpoint diversity. The substantive challenge set
therefore remains 24 authored cases in 12 purposive families,
rather than 15,360 independent tasks.
B. Recovery, harms, and the strong comparison
Every readback policy achieved operational safe recovery in
1,792/3,072 worlds (14/24 cases), compared with 1,024/3,072
(8/24) for response-driven retry. The predeclared equal-family
primary difference was +0.25. Figure 2 shows all 12 family
contrasts: three were +1, and nine were zero. Differences
against the reference, state-only, and one-final-read policies
were zero in every family.
The three positive families have specific explanations. In
weather-supply and translation-routing cases, response-driven
timeout retries created an extra non-idempotent effect; the
readback policies dispatched once. In mobile-lab cases, the
first response explicitly terminated without an effect; readback
policies verified fresh absence and retried, while the response
baseline’s declared error rule stopped. This third gain concerns
that rule, rather than superior deduplication. The equally
informed classical reference achieved the same recovery as
the package.
Residual harm is material. In the same 256 archive-rights
worlds, one initial provider dispatch created two wrong-
payload effects. These are both duplicate and unwanted events,
not 512 distinct harmed worlds. Detection did not cancel or
repair the effects. Environmental-release cases supply a dif-
ferent failure: an external actor satisfies the original contract,
then a delayed own effect breaks the later exact-count target
without an own duplicate or unwanted flag. All 256 response
worlds with original satisfaction but failed recovery belong to
this family. Equal aggregate original/recovery counts for the
readback policies conceal a 256-world later-recovery gain and
a different 256-world later loss. One target cannot substitute
for the other.
All 3,072 reference–polling pairs agreed on final
state/linkage reports, endpoint vectors, controller action
sequences, and full retained truth. This is observed
implementation conformance under the shared policy,
not superiority over the strong reference. State-only and
polling also had identical actions and physical histories;
1,024 pairs differed in the action-linked report. Provenance
expanded reporting here, without a recovery-action benefit.
C. Reporting and the cost of additional polls
The response policy made 768 false-success and 256
false-negative state reports. The readback policies made no
wrong definite state report, but abstained in 768/3,072 worlds.
Provenance-aware policies retained 1,024/3,072 linked absten-
tions; state-only retained 2,048/3,072. Correct negative reports
contribute to these yields. Zero wrong original-contract reports
did not imply zero harms or guaranteed safe recovery.
Polling and one-final-read agreed on both final claims, dis-
patches, effect histories, and every non-polling-cost endpoint
in all 3,072 pairs. Polling nevertheless consumed 7,680 reads
versus 3,840, with mean logical decision time 220 versus
200 ms. These are simulated decision times, not measured
production latency. The schedule explains the negative finding:
no case with insufficient evidence at 𝐻 became admissibly
known at 𝐻 + 40 or 𝐻 + 80. Transient limitations either
cleared before the first final read or persisted beyond the
last opportunity. Thus Study A shows no incremental polling
utility under its schedules; it does not test the general benefit
of recoverable lag. The separately frozen follow-up below
addresses that specific coverage gap.

B. Matched conditions and audit boundary
The prospective design selects 12 distinct airline task IDs by
a declared metadata-based hash rule, excluding development
IDs 19 and 20. Six tasks have substantive communication
criteria and six have empty criteria. Four conditions and one
declared seed give 48 planned trajectories, not 48 independent
tasks. Task and rotating condition order must be published with
the execution protocol before official generation. The corpus
was exposed in the earlier model-free transfer study; these are
externally authored, previously exposed tasks, not an author-
unseen holdout.
All conditions retain the complete native policy, tool
schemas, actual environment, dynamic user, and required DB
plus COMMUNICATE reward. Proposed mutations determine
a shared public-read schedule; subsequent dialogue and evi-
dence can diverge. Figure 4 identifies the four conditions. C
and G share both the application predicate and conservative
guard. G invokes that predicate through a custom ConseqaX
2.1.1 verifier and emits a full receipt, while C emits a compact
report. Their comparison tests integration, representation and
guard interactions, not independent verifier arithmetic or the
built-in temporal RecordVerifier. Shared predicate errors
can survive automated agreement. The guard can suppress a
legitimate later identical request and may never activate in
ordinary dialogue; either outcome must be retained.
The planned shared ceilings are 80 native steps, 48 com-
bined assistant/user model invocations, 512 generated tokens
per request, and the amended request/trajectory deadlines
above, with no corrective retries. Every generation must
pass a complete-history render, token-ID/context check and
model-identity check without history trimming. Local request
normalization is restricted to the pinned provider’s disclosed
schema serialization and ordinary assistant null-field omission.
A failed gate stops that trajectory; no replacement completion
is silently substituted.
The primary finite-pilot endpoint is operational full native
reward over all 12 planned slots per condition. Failed or
unstarted slots cannot earn operational success. Unavailable
native reward and components remain unavailable; native
premature termination retains its zero reward with missing
component grades. Empty communication criteria pass vac-
uously only when component evaluation is reached. A passed
artifact audit does not imply that all planned slots ran or
that any task succeeded. The audit must retain every planned
disposition and distinguish complete accounting from observed
journal prefixes or missing usage.
A separately implemented auditor imports neither the col-
lection runner, wrapper, transport nor framework. It recon-
structs all actual calls chronologically and regrades saved
native trajectories through the unchanged evaluator. The addi-
tional read replay matters because native strict replay skips
nonmutating reads. This is automated checking within the
same AI-assisted project, not independent external replication.
Native synchronous execution injects no lost acknowledgement
or delayed commit. Current public fields do not establish
temporal durability, settlement, safe retry, or action origin;
receipt origin remains unknown. The same small model sup-
plies assistant and user, one seed does not estimate execution
variance, and persistent prompt caches plus shared host load
prevent causal wall-time attribution to the package.
VII. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE AND VERSION BOUNDARIES
Earlier experiments corroborate narrower components with-
out adding independent recovery families. Version 1.1.0 re-
tained 503/504 factual-audit episodes across three small local
models, with 72 unknown state reports; an 84-trace same-
evidence judge made nine wrong among 60 definite state
reports. Model-free tool transfer retained 656 cells and 900
operation captures, without autonomous reward. The native
CAR bridge matched eligible point vectors in 640/1,024 paired
groups; 384 were unavailable [ 17], [ 18]. Version 2.0 offline
migration preserved all 503 retained canonical conclusions
while changing 275 diagnosis codes. The historical manuscript
and versioned evidence ledger retain denominators, protocol
deviations, and detailed results.
The new experiment uses selected 2.1.1 public recording,
contract, observation, and record-verification paths [ 7]. Its
callback-based application polling and safety gate are explicit
integration code. It does not evaluate the public blocking
polling helper, composite/multiple-source verification, Open-
Telemetry, SDK integrations, arbitrary multi-action traces, or
the full release surface. Historical 1.1.0 results and 2.0 mi-
gration are not relabeled as 2.1.1 performance. Byte-identical
numerical modules alone would not establish end-to-end ver-
sion equivalence.

Strong-reference parity should be interpreted directly. Sep-
arate arithmetic reduces reliance on one verifier implemen-
tation, but a shared controller author and application state
machine can preserve shared mistakes. The saved-artifact
auditor uses separate code, while its author also implemented
the controllers; this is not external replication. Exact agreement
on this suite is neither superiority nor a proof of general safety.
Advantages over response or state ablations must identify the
omitted evidence and the precise claim or decision it changes.
If provenance changes only reporting, that is the measured
boundary; a causal action benefit cannot be inferred.
The controlled recovery corpus is small, purposive, AI-
authored, and implemented on one local substrate. Three
record domains broaden application vocabulary and predicate
use without establishing transfer to independent services. Re-
peated timing seeds cannot repair this external-validity gap.
Deterministic policies exercise recovery logic, not language
understanding, user negotiation, planning, provider authentica-
tion, or autonomous tool selection. These data can characterize
a conditional decision boundary; they cannot establish general
autonomous-agent improvement or effectiveness across inde-
pendently operated services.
Evaluation uses executable application contracts, effect-
ledger ground truth, and separately implemented automated
checks. No participant study or annotation study is part
of this paper; interpretability, preference, rationale quality,
and user trust are not measured. Automated cross-checks
can expose implementation or arithmetic inconsistencies, but
cannot establish that an external adapter’s source-admission
declarations or provider guarantees are true. That requires
objective validation against the service’s actual semantics and
independently retained effects.
The pending native comparison extends execution to an
externally authored environment but does not inject ambigu-
ous commits. The next fault-focused validation should freeze
selection and a real agent policy in an external benchmark or
provider sandbox, preserving strong classical recovery controls
under identical capabilities and budgets. Native reward must
accompany independently checked effect histories and vali-
dation of adapter/status/idempotency semantics. The supplied
strong guards and committed-effect grading of LIMBO [ 6]
are relevant comparators, rather than novelty claims for this
framework. A declared sampling design and precision target
are necessary for broader inference. More internal cases, AI
audits, or reference-action replay cannot substitute for this
validation.
IX. CONCLUSION AND ARTIFACT A VAILABILITY
ConseqaX provides inspectable boundaries between tool
responses, verified temporal state, operation-linked origin,
and explicitly recorded assertions and evidence. The closed-
loop study evaluates the application decisions and resource
costs that follow from those boundaries under shared provider
guarantees. Its scope is a frozen challenge suite and determin-
istic recovery policies. Historical model, transfer, replay, and
migration evidence retains its original versions and targets.

Comparisons use established provider guarantees as shared
assumptions and treat reference agreement as conformance.
The current MIT distribution is version 2.1.2 [ 19]; these
experiments retain their 2.1.1 pin [ 7]. The public repository
retains protocols, source hashes, schedules, raw traces, dis-
positions, evaluator arithmetic, and deviations. A DOI-backed
immutable research archive has not yet been established. The
earlier manuscript preserves full replay definitions, interval
tradeoffs, source pins, and historical audits. No real payment
or production-service write is part of the reported recovery
experiments.
